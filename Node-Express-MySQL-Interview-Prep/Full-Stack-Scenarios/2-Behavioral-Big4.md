# Big4 Behavioral Interview Guide
**Frequency: ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ — PwC/Deloitte/EY weight this EQUALLY with technical**

Big4 consulting firms are different from product companies. They sell your expertise to clients,
so they evaluate: communication, client-impact mindset, adaptability, and professionalism.

---

## The STAR Method (Non-negotiable)

**S** — Situation: Context (project, team, timeline)  
**T** — Task: Your specific responsibility  
**A** — Action: What YOU specifically did (not "we")  
**R** — Result: Measurable outcome + what you learned  

**Key**: Big4 wants IMPACT stories. "Reduced page load by 40%" > "improved performance."

---

## Core Questions and Model Answers

### "Tell me about yourself" (Opening — must be perfect)

Structure: Current role → Relevant skills → Why this role

> "I'm a fullstack developer with 2+ years of experience building React/Node.js applications.
> I've worked on [specific product], where I built [specific feature] that [measurable impact].
> I'm particularly interested in [company]'s work in [industry/practice area] because [specific reason].
> I'm looking to apply my technical skills in a consulting context where I can solve complex
> problems for diverse clients."

---

### "Describe a challenging technical problem you solved"

**What they're evaluating**: Technical depth + structured thinking + communication

Template answer:
> "While building [feature] for [project], I encountered [problem].
> I analyzed the issue by [diagnostic approach], identified that [root cause].
> I evaluated [option A] vs [option B], chose [option] because [reasoning].
> I implemented [solution], which resulted in [measurable outcome].
> I documented the approach so the team could handle similar issues."

**Prep your story**: Performance bottleneck, difficult bug, ambiguous requirement, 
competing priorities, team disagreement on approach.

---

### "How do you handle a situation where requirements change mid-project?"

**Big4 context**: This happens constantly in consulting. They want to see adaptability.

> "I've learned to expect requirement changes, especially in client-facing projects.
> When [specific situation] occurred, I first assessed the impact on existing work.
> I communicated clearly with stakeholders about trade-offs and timeline.
> I [specific action taken to adapt]. The outcome was [result].
> Now I build with flexibility in mind — modular code, clear separation of concerns."

---

### "Tell me about a time you worked with a difficult team member"

**What they're evaluating**: Professionalism, communication, conflict resolution

> "On [project], I worked with a colleague who had very different ideas about [technical approach].
> Rather than escalating, I requested a 1:1 to understand their perspective.
> I found [specific valid point in their view]. We then agreed on [compromise].
> The result was actually better than either original approach.
> I learned that technical disagreements are often communication gaps."

---

### "Where do you see yourself in 5 years?"

**Big4 context**: They want to hear career progression within consulting.

> "I want to grow from a technical contributor to a technical lead who bridges client needs 
> with technical solutions. At [company], I'd aim to develop deep expertise in 
> [practice area — cloud/digital transformation/fintech] and eventually guide clients
> through architecture decisions, not just implementation.
> I see [company]'s [specific service/industry] practice as the right environment for that."

---

### "Why Big4 over a product company?"

> "Product companies optimize for one product. In consulting, I'd work across
> [industries] — financial services, healthcare, government — solving different
> architectural challenges with each engagement. That breadth of exposure accelerates
> learning in a way that a single product team doesn't.
> I also find the client-impact model compelling — seeing how technical solutions
> directly affect a client's business outcomes, not just internal metrics."

## Technical Communication — What Big4 Expects

### Explaining technical decisions to non-technical stakeholders

> BAD: "We used Redis with a sliding window rate limiter and JWT rotation."
> GOOD: "We added a system that prevents our login from being attacked by bots,
>        and made sure users stay logged in securely without security risks."

Practice translating every technical decision into business language.

### Code Review Communication

When reviewing someone's code at Big4:
- Frame feedback as questions: "Have you considered...?" vs "This is wrong"
- Acknowledge what's good first
- Reference standards/docs, not personal preference
- Big4 works with client teams — diplomatic communication is professional currency

## Big4-Specific Preparation Points

### PwC — Technology & Digital Transformation
- Focus: Cloud migrations (AWS/Azure), digital transformation programs
- Expect: System design for legacy → cloud migration, microservices vs monolith
- Value: Enterprise architecture knowledge, team leadership potential

### Deloitte — Technology Consulting
- Focus: Enterprise applications, SAP, Salesforce, custom development
- Expect: Deep SDLC knowledge, Agile/Scrum, stakeholder management
- Value: Can work directly with client CTOs, present technical proposals

### EY — Technology Risk + Transformation
- Focus: Financial services tech, data analytics, security/compliance
- Expect: GDPR/HIPAA awareness, audit trails, security patterns
- Value: Risk awareness, documentation discipline, regulated industry experience

## Questions to Ask the Interviewer

These signal consulting mindset:
1. "What does a typical client engagement look like for a developer at this level?"
2. "How does the team balance between client work and internal tooling/upskilling?"
3. "What's the most technically complex project this practice has handled recently?"
4. "How do you handle knowledge transfer at the end of an engagement?"

**Avoid**: "What are the benefits?" or "How many vacation days?" — save for HR.

## Pre-Interview Checklist

### Technical
- [ ] 2-3 system design scenarios ready (with trade-offs)
- [ ] 3-5 coding challenge patterns fresh (not just theory)
- [ ] Your portfolio projects: can explain every technical decision
- [ ] Know your resume tech stack deeply — they ask deep dive on anything listed

### Behavioral
- [ ] 5 STAR stories prepared (technical challenge, team conflict, failure, success, leadership)
- [ ] Company research: recent projects, practice areas, client industries
- [ ] Understand the role level (associate, consultant, senior consultant)
- [ ] Questions prepared for each interviewer (technical + cultural)

### Day-of
- [ ] Camera/audio tested for video interviews
- [ ] IDE ready with your preferred language configured
- [ ] Whiteboard/paper for system design
- [ ] LinkedIn profile updated (they check during/after interview)

## Salary Negotiation (Big4 Context)

Big4 salary bands are public knowledge (Glassdoor/Levels.fyi). They are less negotiable
than product companies but signing bonuses and relocation are negotiable.

- Lead with value: "Based on my experience with [X, Y, Z], I'm targeting the senior band"
- Know the band: Research current market rates for your city/level
- Total comp: Factor in training budget, certification reimbursement, professional development
